# Base vans: generations, engines, known faults

For the reviewer. Ratings: PREFER / OK / CAUTION / AVOID. (verify) = sources disagree or unconfirmed. Any engine with no timing or injector history at >200k km is one level worse.

## Fiat Ducato / Citroën Jumper / Peugeot Boxer (same body; FWD)
Generations: X244 2002-2006 | X250 2006-2014 | X290 2014-2021 | X290 facelift 2021+
- All gens: rust at sills under the A-pillar, wheel-arch transitions, door bottoms, body mounts. Rear leaf springs and dampers are overloaded by heavy builds and rear garages. FWD loses traction on wet grass/sand with a heavy rear.
- X250 gearbox (M40, 6-speed): 5th/6th bearing whine; reverse judder on inclines ("characteristic", worse on 6-speed); corroded gear linkage on top of the box (hard to select reverse); clutch + dual-mass flywheel wear around ~120k km in heavy vans.
- X250: blocked scuttle drain lets water into the engine bay/footwells.

Fiat engines:
- 2.8 JTD (Iveco 8140, X244): durable, old. OK if rust-free.
- 2.3 MultiJet (Iveco F1A; 110-150 KM X250, 130-180 KM X290): OK/PREFER. Timing BELT on an interference engine (snapped belt = engine destroyed); interval is km AND years, and campers hit the years first. Ask for the receipt or budget it immediately. EGR soot ~80-120k km; injector copper seals leak ~100-150k km; DPF clogs with short trips; Euro 5 turbo oil-seal leaks after ~150k km.
- 3.0 MultiJet (Iveco F1C; 157-177 KM): PREFER, most durable Ducato engine. One workshop source says timing chain (verify belt vs chain per VIN; ask for timing history either way). EGR and injector seal leaks.
- 2.3 Euro 6 / 6d (2016+, 6d-temp from ~2019): OK. AdBlue/SCR faults, NOx sensors, EGR cooler leaks (coolant loss with no visible leak).
- 2.2 MultiJet 3 (2021+): too new for a track record (verify).
- Comfort-Matic robotized manual (X250/X290 to 2021): AVOID. Slow, jerky; clutch adaptation issues.
- ZF 9-speed torque-converter automatic (2021+): OK/PREFER, smooth. Change the oil early with heavy loads and mountains.

Citroën/Peugeot engines:
- 2.2 HDi "Puma" (Ford Duratorq, 100-130 KM, 2006-~2011): CAUTION. Timing chain stretch/tensioner (rattle on cold start), injectors, EGR, DPF (Euro 5). Weak for a heavy camper.
- 3.0 HDi (same Iveco F1C, 157 KM): PREFER.
- 2.0 BlueHDi (2014+) / 2.2 BlueHDi (later): OK. Timing BELT (one source: 180k km or 10 years, verify). AdBlue: NOx sensors, frozen or crystallised injector.

Verdict: 3.0 > 2.3 > BlueHDi > 2.2 HDi Puma. Avoid Comfort-Matic. X244 only if exceptionally rust-free.

## Mercedes Sprinter (RWD; 4x4 rare)
Generations: T1N W901-905 1995-2006 | W906 2006-2018 (facelift 2013) | W907/VS30 2018+ (adds FWD)
- T1N: very rust-prone (sills, arches, sliding door, chassis). OM611/612/647 CDI very durable; injector seal leaks ("black death" carbon); 5th gear bearing whine.
- W906 rust: arches, sills, door bottoms, rear crossmember, jacking points, windscreen frame. Worst on 2006-2010.
- W906 electronic steering lock (ELV/ESL): stiff key, then "steering lock malfunction" and no start. Fix before long trips (~0.5-2k PLN).
- W906 other: glow plug control unit, sliding door rollers, leaf springs cracking under load.

Engines:
- OM646 2.1 CDI (2148 cm3, ~2006-2009; 109-150 KM): PREFER. Front timing chain, simple, robust. DPF/EGR on later Euro 4.
- OM651 2.1 CDI (2143 cm3, 2009-2018+; 210 = 95 KM, 213/313 = 129 KM, 216/316 = 163 KM): OK. Timing chain at the REAR (engine out, ~4-6.5k PLN) stretches with long oil intervals; check the camshaft deviation in Xentry. Early Delphi injectors (2009-~2013) leak and stick; a failed injector can wash a cylinder. DPF/EGR, oil dilution after failed regenerations. Upper tensioner can be changed from the top (cheap preventive fix).
- OM642 3.0 V6 (318/319 CDI): OK. Oil cooler seal leaks, swirl flaps (verify); costly but powerful. Rare in PL.
- OM654 2.0 (W907, ~2019+): OK. AdBlue/NOx sensor faults ("start not possible in x km"), oil separator leaks.
- Gearboxes: 6-speed manual OK; 7G-Tronic (W906) OK; 9G-Tronic (W907): early harsh 1st-3rd shifts, usually fixed by software.

Verdict: W906 OM646 (rust permitting) or OM651 2011+ with chain and injector history. W907 = best but expensive.

## Renault Master / Opel Movano / Nissan NV400 (mostly FWD; RWD twin-wheel versions exist)
Generations: Master II 1997-2010 | Master III 2010-2019 (facelift 2019-2024) | Master IV 2024+
- Master II (1.9/2.2 G9T/2.5 G9U dCi): CAUTION. PF6 gearbox bearing failure (whine in 5th/6th) is the biggest issue; EGR, turbos (G9T/G9U), injectors; heavy rust (sills, arches, outriggers).
- Master III 2.3 dCi M9T: PREFER. Timing chain (rattle = wear); EGR, EGR cooler leaks, DPF and oil dilution; drop links. Single turbo 2010-2014, twin-turbo "Energy" from 2014 (verify).
- Master III facelift 2019+: AdBlue the most reported fault, NOx sensors, stop-start.
- Quickshift robotized manual: AVOID/CAUTION. Clutch actuator, adaptation needs Renault Clip, jerky starts.

Verdict: Master III manual with M9T is a good value base; avoid Quickshift and rusty Master II.

## Iveco Daily (ladder frame, RWD; harsh ride, heavy)
Generations: Daily II 1999-2006 | 2006-2014 | Daily III 2014-2019 | facelift 2019+
- 2.3 HPI (F1A): OK. Belt-driven; EGR clogging; turbo actuator failures (2014-2019).
- 3.0 HPT (F1C): PREFER. EGR, injector seal blow-by (fuel smell in the cab).
- 2006-2014 has the most emissions faults (EGR, DPF, injector seals, ABS sensors). 2019+: AdBlue/NOx.
- Hi-Matic 8-speed ZF auto (2014+): well regarded (verify).
- Frame-mounted body = robust, but the kerb weight eats payload; check DMC (3,500 vs 3,500+).

## Ford Transit (not sampled in our listings)
- Mk7 2006-2014: 2.2 TDCi FWD, 2.4 RWD, 3.2 5-cyl. Duratorq injectors, DMF, rust at arches and sills. OK.
- Mk8 2014+: 2.2 TDCi to ~2016 (OK); 2.0 EcoBlue 2016+ CAUTION. Wet timing belt runs in oil; fuel dilution degrades it, debris blocks the oil pump strainer, failure = engine loss. Replace belt + oil pump belt on schedule; smell the oil for diesel.

## VW Crafter / MAN TGE (not sampled)
- Gen 1 2006-2016 = Sprinter W906 body, same rust (rear arches, sliding door sill, rear crossmember).
  - 2.5 TDI R5 (2006-2011): CAUTION. Early turbo failures (largely fixed in a 2010 revision), DPF, injector wear.
  - 2.0 TDI (2011-2016): OK. EGR, injector seals, occasional turbo.
- Gen 2 2017+ (VW-built; FWD/RWD/4Motion; TGE twin): early 2.0 TDI injection teething; 2019+ more reliable.
- Both gens: dual-mass flywheel (rattle at idle with the clutch engaged).

## Quick lookup for the reviewer
- AVOID: Comfort-Matic, Quickshift, rust-holed T1N / Master II / X244, any van >400k km with no invoiced rebuild.
- CAUTION: 2.2 HDi Puma, Crafter 2.5 TDI pre-2010, Transit 2.0 EcoBlue without belt history, OM651 with no chain/injector history.
- PREFER: Ducato/Jumper/Boxer 3.0, Sprinter OM646 or documented OM651, Master III M9T manual, Daily 3.0.
- Power: <=110 KM is weak for a 3,500 kg camper; 130+ KM is comfortable, 150+ for L4 or mountains.
- In Polish registration documents, power (P.2) is in kW: 70 kW = 95 KM, 95 kW = 129 KM, 96 kW = 130 KM, 110 kW = 150 KM, 120 kW = 163 KM.
